Asphalt Calculator

How it works

volume = length × width × depth, then weight = volume × density, then the waste allowance is added. With the default 145 lb/ft³, one square yard at one inch deep weighs about 109 lb, which is why paving crews quote the rule of thumb of roughly 110 lb per square yard per inch. One US ton is 2,000 lb; one metric tonne is 1,000 kg (about 1.10 US tons).

Depth guidelines

Residential driveway: 2–3 in of finish course over a compacted 4–8 in gravel base. Parking lot: 3–4 in over 6–8 in of base. Heavy-truck roads and industrial yards: 4 in or more over an engineered base. Do not lay asphalt directly over uncompacted soil; the base matters more than the surface for long-term durability. Enter the compacted depth, since loose mix is spread thicker and rolled down.

Ordering and delivery

Asphalt cools fast, so it is delivered hot and must be spread and compacted right away; you can't stage it. Plants usually have a minimum order (often 2–5 tons) and set delivery windows. Prices vary a lot by region and season, so get more than one quote.

Basis: 145 lb/ft³ is a common estimating density for dense-graded hot-mix asphalt (typical range 140–150 lb/ft³); the ~110 lb/yd²-per-inch spread figure is the matching field shortcut. Checked November 2026.

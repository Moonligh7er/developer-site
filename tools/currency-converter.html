Currency Converter

Convert between the 30 currencies the European Central Bank publishes daily reference rates for. Rates are fetched live when you open the page, and every result shows which day's rates it used.

Rates come from the free Frankfurter API (api.frankfurter.dev), which republishes the ECB's data. The page downloads the whole rate table in one request, so nothing you type — not the amount, not the currencies you pick — is sent anywhere. The table (public data only) is kept in your browser for six hours so repeat visits load instantly.

How this converter works

When the page opens it asks the free, no-key Frankfurter API for the ECB's latest reference rates, all quoted against the euro. Any pair is then a cross-rate through the euro: USD → GBP = (EUR→GBP) ÷ (EUR→USD). If the API can't be reached, the page falls back to the last table saved in your browser and says how old it is; if there is none, it tells you so rather than showing a made-up number. Results are rounded to each currency's usual decimals (none for yen or won, two for most others).

Why ECB reference rates

The European Central Bank publishes reference rates around 16:00 CET on working days. They are a neutral, transparent mid-market snapshot, but they are not live trading rates and not what your bank or card will give you. Banks and card networks add a margin (often 1–3%) and sometimes a fee; transfer services publish their own rates, usually close to mid-market.

Weekends and holidays

On weekends and ECB holidays there's no new rate, so the latest business day's rates are used. The date under the result always tells you which day's rates you're looking at.

What this converter doesn't do

Real-time trading rates, cryptocurrency, currencies outside the ECB's list, historical charts or forecasts. For a rough “how much is 500 dollars in yen” it's a good fit; for an actual transfer, check the rate and fees your provider quotes.

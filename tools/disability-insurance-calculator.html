How it works: the 60–70% guideline

Planners often suggest a disability benefit of 60–70% of gross income. Insurers rarely sell more than that, so you keep a reason to return to work, and an individually bought policy pays benefits tax-free, which makes 60–70% of gross land close to typical take-home pay. So the calculator treats the target as a tax-free monthly amount: your target % × gross income.

Your group LTD benefit is the plan % × income, limited by the plan maximum. If the employer pays the premium (or you pay it pre-tax), the benefit is taxable, so the calculator reduces it by your tax rate to compare like with like. Other after-tax income is then added, and the shortfall against the target is the coverage gap. A second line compares the same total with your essential expenses.

Group LTD details to check

Look for the monthly maximum (often $10,000–$15,000, which caps the benefit for high earners), the definition of disability (own-occupation pays if you can't do your job; any-occupation only if you can't do any job you're suited for, which is a much higher bar, and many group plans switch to it after two years), offsets (most group plans reduce the benefit by Social Security disability and similar payments), and the elimination period (the wait before benefits start, commonly 90–180 days, so an emergency fund matters).

Filling a gap

An individual disability policy can sit on top of group coverage. These are often own-occupation, non-cancelable and portable between jobs, and they are priced on age and health, so they cost less when bought younger. High earners such as physicians often need one to reach 60% replacement because of group caps.

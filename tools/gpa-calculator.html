GPA Calculator

GPA on the common US 4.0 scale from your courses, credits and letter grades. It shows the unweighted GPA, a weighted GPA when you mark honors or AP/IB courses, and a new cumulative GPA if you add your previous totals. Results update as you type.

Scale used: A+ and A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, D− = 0.7, F = 0. Schools differ (some give A+ 4.3 or use other steps), so check your school's scale.

How it works

GPA is a credit-weighted average: add up grade points × credits for each course (“quality points”) and divide by the total credits. A 1-credit course moves your GPA less than a 4-credit course with the same grade. Rows without both credits and a grade are left out, and the result says so.

The scale is the common US 4.0 scale above, with plus/minus steps of 0.3 and A+ counted the same as A. Some colleges give A+ 4.3, some don't use minus grades for D, and other countries use different systems entirely.

Weighted GPA

Many US high schools add 0.5 for honors courses and 1.0 for AP/IB courses, so an A in AP Chemistry counts as 5.0. Here the bonus applies to passing grades only (an F stays 0), which is the most common rule, but policies vary: some schools don't add it to D grades either. Colleges often recalculate an unweighted GPA from your transcript.

Cumulative GPA

New cumulative = (previous GPA × previous credits + this term's quality points) ÷ (previous credits + this term's credits). Use a previous GPA on the same basis: the unweighted line uses this term's unweighted points, the weighted line its weighted points.

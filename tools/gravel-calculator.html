How it works

volume = length × width × depth × (1 + extra), then weight = cubic yards × tons per cubic yard. 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ = 0.7646 m³, and 1 US ton = 2,000 lb = 0.907 tonnes, so 1 ton/yd³ equals about 1.19 t/m³.

Typical densities used

  • Pea gravel and clean #57 crushed stone: about 1.4 tons/yd³ (2,800 lb; 1.66 t/m³).
  • Crusher run / road base with fines: about 1.5 tons/yd³ loose; heavier once compacted.
  • River rock: about 1.3 tons/yd³. Decomposed granite: about 1.35 tons/yd³, but sources range from about 1.1 to 1.5.
  • Lava rock (scoria) is porous and light: about 0.7 tons/yd³ (1,300–1,500 lb).

How gravel is sold

By volume (cubic yards or cubic meters, delivered by truck) or by weight (tons, delivered or loaded into your truck). If your supplier sells by the ton, use the weight line; by the yard, use the volume line. Bags are usually 0.5 ft³ (about 50 lb), so one cubic yard is 54 bags.

Depth guidelines

Driveways: a 4 in compacted base layer plus a 2–3 in top layer. Walking paths: 2–3 in over landscape fabric. Drainage under pavers or slabs: 4–6 in of clean crushed stone. If the depth varies, use the average.

Basis: typical loose densities from supplier and quarry planning figures (#57 stone 1.35–1.5 tons/yd³). Checked November 2026.

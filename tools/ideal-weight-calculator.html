What “ideal weight” formulas actually are

Devine (1974) proposed his formula in a paper on dosing the antibiotic gentamicin. Robinson (1983) and Miller (1983) published re-fits in a hospital-pharmacy journal, again for drug-dose calculations. Hamwi (1964) came from diabetes diet planning. All four take height as the only input and produce one number, with nothing for frame size, muscle or body composition.

Because height is all they use, they tend to undershoot for muscular people and say little about anyone's health. A 6'0" powerlifter and a 6'0" office worker get the same answer from every formula, even though their healthy ranges can differ a lot.

How it works

Each formula starts from a base weight at 5 ft (152.4 cm) and adds a fixed amount per inch above that. Male / female: Devine 50 / 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch; Robinson 52 + 1.9 / 49 + 1.7 kg; Miller 56.2 + 1.41 / 53.1 + 1.36 kg; Hamwi 106 + 6 / 100 + 5 lb per inch (its original units). Below 5 ft the formulas aren't defined, so the tool shows only the BMI-based range. The BMI range is 18.5 × height(m)² to 24.9 × height(m)².

More useful references

For a fuller picture, pair body fat percentage with BMI. Waist-to-height ratio (waist ÷ height, with under 0.5 a commonly used marker) is simple and in some studies tracks heart and metabolic risk better than BMI. DEXA scans measure body composition directly.

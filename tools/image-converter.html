Which format when

PNG: lossless. Best for screenshots, diagrams, images with sharp edges or text, and anything that needs transparency. Files are much bigger than JPEG for photographs.

JPEG: lossy. Best for photographs when file size matters and you don't need transparency. The old standard that works everywhere. Quality 80–90 is hard to tell from the original for most viewers.

WebP: Google's format with lossy and lossless modes plus transparency. Typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at similar quality, and every current browser displays it (Safari since 2020). Some older desktop software still can't open it.

AVIF: the newest of the four, based on the AV1 video codec, and often smaller still than WebP. Current browsers can display it, but fewer can create it, which this page needs; the option is switched off where yours can't.

Transparency, animation and quality

JPEG can't store transparency, so converting a see-through PNG or WebP to JPEG fills those areas with a colour you choose (white by default). PNG, WebP and AVIF keep transparency. Animated GIFs and WebPs keep only their first frame. Converting a lossy file (JPEG) to PNG doesn't bring back lost detail; it just stores what's left without further loss. Converting lossy to lossy (JPEG to WebP) loses a little more each time, so convert from the best original you have. Colour profiles are converted to standard sRGB, and the photo's rotation tag is applied so pictures stay upright.

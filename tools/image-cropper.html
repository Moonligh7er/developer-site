Image Cropper

Crop with a draggable box, freeform or locked to a ratio (1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, 3:2, 2:3, 4:5, passport 2×2″). Drag with a mouse or finger, nudge with the arrow keys, or type exact pixel values. Downloads in the original format where possible, or PNG, JPEG or WebP.

Files stay in your browser. Everything on this page — loading, processing, encoding — runs locally. Your images are never uploaded to any server. Re-encoding also strips EXIF metadata (camera model, GPS coordinates), which is a privacy win.

Using the cropper

Open an image and a box appears over the middle of it. Drag inside the box to move it; drag a corner or edge handle to resize. With a ratio chosen, resizing keeps that ratio. You can also tab to the box and use the arrow keys (Shift + arrows to resize), or type the exact left, top, width and height in original-image pixels. The download is cropped from the full-resolution image, not the on-screen preview.

Common ratios

1:1 for profile photos and square posts. 4:5 for Instagram portrait posts. 4:3 for many phone and compact-camera photos. 3:2 for 35 mm and most DSLR photos and 4×6 prints. 16:9 for HD video, YouTube thumbnails and most screens. 9:16 for vertical video (TikTok, Reels, Stories). 2:3 for portrait prints. 2×2 inch passport: this preset only enforces the square shape. US passport photos also need the head 1–1 3/8 inches tall and, for digital submissions, at least 600×600 pixels — check the current rules for your country, and use the Image Resizer afterwards to hit an exact pixel size.

Formats

By default the crop is saved in the same format as the original where your browser can write it (JPEG and WebP at quality 92), and as PNG otherwise. PNG keeps every pixel exactly but makes large files from photos. Transparent areas stay transparent in PNG and WebP; for JPEG you pick the fill colour. Phone photos are turned upright using their rotation tag before you crop.

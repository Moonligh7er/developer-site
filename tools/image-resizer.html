Image Resizer

Resize by pixel dimensions, by percentage, or to fit under a file-size limit. Target-size mode searches for the highest quality that fits, and shrinks the dimensions only if it has to. Aspect-ratio lock is on by default; presets crop to fill so nothing gets stretched.

Files stay in your browser. Everything on this page — loading, processing, encoding — runs locally. Your images are never uploaded to any server. Re-encoding also strips EXIF metadata (camera model, GPS coordinates), which is a privacy win.

Three modes, one use case each

Pixel dimensions: when you need an exact width or height. With “Keep proportions” on, the other side is worked out for you (width × original height ÷ original width, rounded). Presets set an exact box; with proportions off you choose what happens when the shapes differ: crop to fill from the centre (the default), fit inside the box, or stretch.

Percentage: “half the size” without doing the maths. 50% halves each side, so the image has a quarter of the pixels.

Target file size: for hard limits (email attachments, upload caps, avatars). The tool binary-searches the JPEG/WebP/AVIF quality setting for the largest file at or under your target. If even the lowest quality is too big, it shrinks the dimensions and searches again, and tells you the size and quality it settled on. PNG has no quality setting, so it isn't offered in this mode.

Upscaling caveats

Making an image larger than its original doesn't add detail — the browser invents pixels by interpolation. That's fine for modest upscales (1.5–2×) but looks soft beyond that. Large reductions are done in halving steps, which keeps fine detail cleaner than a single jump.

Quality drops with each re-save

Every time you re-save a JPEG or WebP, lossy compression is applied again. Resize once from your original; if you need several sizes, resize the original each time rather than an already-resized copy. Transparent areas are kept for PNG, WebP and AVIF and filled with your chosen colour for JPEG.

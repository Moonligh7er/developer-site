How US federal tax brackets actually work

The most common tax misconception: “If I take that raise it'll bump me into a higher bracket and I'll take home less!” That is not how it works. US federal income tax is marginal: each dollar is taxed at the rate for its bracket, not the whole income at your top rate. A single filer in 2024 pays 10% on the first $11,600, 12% on income from $11,600–$47,150, 22% on income from $47,150–$100,525, and so on. Your marginal rate is the bracket your last dollar sits in; your effective rate is total tax divided by total income — always lower than marginal.

Taxable income vs. gross income

This calculator takes taxable income, which is your gross income after subtracting either the standard deduction ($14,600 single / $29,200 MFJ in 2024) or itemized deductions, plus pre-tax retirement contributions, HSA, etc. If you enter gross income by mistake, results will overstate your tax.

What this doesn't include

FICA (Social Security + Medicare = 7.65% up to wage base), state and local income tax, AMT, capital-gains treatment, credits (Child Tax Credit, EITC, etc.), or itemized-deduction phase-outs. This is federal ordinary-income tax only. For a full picture, use tax-prep software or a CPA.

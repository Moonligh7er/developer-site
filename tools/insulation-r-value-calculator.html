ENERGY STAR recommended levels

For existing wood-framed homes, ENERGY STAR recommends adding insulation to these levels. The figures are based on the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code (Table R402.1.3).

Walls: when siding comes off, ENERGY STAR suggests blowing insulation into empty cavities and adding R-5 insulative sheathing in zone 3, or R-5 to R-10 in zones 4–8. Wall cavities are limited by stud depth: 2×4 walls hold R-13 to R-15 batts, 2×6 walls R-19 to R-21. Find your zone and ZIP-specific advice on ENERGY STAR's insulation page.

How it works

depth = R-value ÷ R per inch. Batts come at fixed R-values, so the calculator stacks layers of the product you pick until it reaches the target, then divides the total batt area by the package coverage. Blown-in bags cover less area as the R-value goes up: bags = area × R ÷ (30 × coverage at R-30), which tracks bag charts closely up to about R-38 and runs slightly optimistic above that. Spray foam is quoted in board feet (1 sq ft at 1 in thick).

Typical R per inch

Fiberglass batts about 2.9–3.8 (high-density up to about 4.3). Mineral wool batts about 3.7–4.3. Blown cellulose about 3.2–3.8 settled. Blown fiberglass about 2.2–2.9. Open-cell spray foam about 3.5–3.8. Closed-cell spray foam about 6–7. Blown-in material settles, so install to the bag's initial thickness to end up at the settled thickness.

Sources: ENERGY STAR “Recommended Home Insulation R-Values” (2021 IECC basis); manufacturer coverage charts for blown cellulose. Checked November 2026.

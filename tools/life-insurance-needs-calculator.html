How it works: DIME vs. a multiple, both shortcuts

The DIME method adds Debt + Income replacement (annual income × years) + Mortgage + Education, plus final expenses, then subtracts existing coverage and savings. It is more specific than a rule of thumb but only as good as each estimate. The income-multiple method is coverage = income × a multiple (10–15× is the usual range), minus the same existing coverage and savings. It is quick but ignores your particular debts and family.

Neither replaces a real needs analysis. When the two disagree by a lot, usually because of a large mortgage or young children, the DIME number tends to be easier to defend. DIME doesn't discount future income for investment growth or inflation, and it replaces your full income even though your own living costs would stop; those two effects partly cancel.

How long you need it matters as much as how much

The need usually shrinks over time as the mortgage is paid down, children finish school and savings grow. Many people don't need permanent coverage for the full DIME amount; a level term policy sized for the peak-need years is often cheaper and simpler. The term vs. whole life comparison walks through that tradeoff.

Get real quotes before deciding

Premiums vary a great deal with age, health class, tobacco use, coverage amount and term length, so any “typical premium” online is illustrative at best. Comparing several quotes, for example through an independent broker who works with multiple insurers, is the reliable way to price it.

How this calculator works

The monthly principal-and-interest payment uses the standard formula for a fixed-rate loan: M = P · r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12), and n is the number of payments (years × 12). As on a lender's schedule, the payment and each month's interest are rounded to the cent, and the last payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly $0.

What is PITI?

PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance, the four parts of a typical monthly mortgage payment when your lender collects taxes and insurance in escrow. Yearly property tax and homeowners insurance are divided by 12 and added to principal and interest. Lenders usually qualify you against the full PITI, not just P&I.

What is PMI?

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender when you borrow more than 80% of the home's value (less than 20% down). It typically costs about 0.3% to 1.5% of the original loan a year, paid monthly. Under the Homeowners Protection Act you can ask the lender to cancel it once the balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it ends automatically at 78% on the original schedule. This calculator charges PMI on each payment while the balance at the start of that month is above 80% of the home price, as if you ask for cancellation as soon as you can.

Do extra payments help?

Usually, yes. Interest is charged on the remaining balance, so every extra dollar of principal stops accruing interest from then on. On a 30-year loan even $100–$200 a month tends to take years off the term. The results show the time, interest and PMI saved for the amount you enter; check that your lender applies extra payments to principal and charges no prepayment penalty.

What this calculator does not include

HOA fees, closing costs, points, adjustable-rate resets, prepayment penalties, escrow shortages, rising tax or insurance bills, and lender-specific fees are not modeled. The output is a clean baseline; a real Loan Estimate from a lender will differ. A planning tool, not a quote.

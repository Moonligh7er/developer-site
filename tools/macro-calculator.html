How it works

Each macro's share of calories is divided by its energy density (the Atwater factors): protein and carbohydrate 4 kcal per gram, fat 9 kcal per gram. A 30/40/30 split of 2,000 kcal is 150 g protein (600 kcal), 200 g carbohydrate (800 kcal) and about 67 g fat (600 kcal). Grams are rounded, so they may add back up to a few kcal off the target. Alcohol (7 kcal/g) and fibre aren't modelled.

When each preset might fit

Balanced 30/40/30: general-purpose eating, maintenance or slow recomposition. Higher protein 40/35/25: often used during a calorie deficit to help hold on to lean mass, or alongside strength training. Lower carb 35/20/45: for people who feel better with fewer carbohydrates; note that 20% is still well above a ketogenic diet, which usually keeps carbohydrate under about 10% of calories. Endurance 25/55/20: for high training volumes where refilling glycogen matters. Individual needs vary.

Protein per body weight

The adult RDA is 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day, a minimum for most healthy adults rather than an optimum. For people doing resistance training, a 2018 meta-analysis (Morton et al., Br J Sports Med) found gains in lean mass levelled off around 1.6 g/kg/day, with 2.2 g/kg as the upper end of the confidence range. Enter your weight above to see where a split lands; if it's far from where you want, switch to custom.

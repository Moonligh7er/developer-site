Percentage Calculator

Five everyday percentage questions on one page. Pick one, type the numbers, and the answer updates as you go, with the working shown.

Processing happens entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Result

The five questions, explained

X% of Y: the most common one. 15% of 200 = 15 × 200 ÷ 100 = 30. Useful for tips, discounts, tax and commissions.

X is what % of Y: the reverse. 45 out of 300 is 45 ÷ 300 × 100 = 15%. Useful for scores, ratios and shares.

Percent change: (new − old) ÷ |old| × 100. From 100 to 125 is +25%; from 100 to 75 is −25%. Change is measured against the starting value, so 50 → 100 is +100% but 100 → 50 is only −50%. Dividing by the size of the old value keeps the sign meaningful when the old value is negative (−50 → −25 is a 50% increase). From zero there is no percent change to report.

Add / take off %: 80 plus 25% = 80 × 1.25 = 100; 80 minus 25% = 80 × 0.75 = 60. Taking off the same percent you added doesn't get you back where you started (100 minus 25% is 75), and the tool shows the percent that would.

X is P% of what: finds the whole from a part. If 30 is 15% of something, the whole is 30 ÷ 15 × 100 = 200.

Percentage points vs. percent

If a rate goes from 4% to 5%, that's a 1 percentage point increase, but a 25% relative increase (1 ÷ 4 = 25%). News stories often mix these up; they mean different things.

Answers are shown to up to 6 decimal places, with trailing zeros dropped; very large or very small results switch to scientific notation.

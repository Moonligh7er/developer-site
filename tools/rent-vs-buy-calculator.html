Rent vs. Buy Calculator

A side-by-side of buying and renting over the years you expect to stay. Both households spend the same cash every month: the buyer pays the mortgage, tax, insurance, upkeep and HOA; the renter pays rent and invests whatever is left over (starting with the down payment and closing costs). At the end, the buyer sells, and the calculator compares what each one has.

How it works

The naive comparison, “$2,200 rent vs. a $2,000 mortgage, so buying wins”, leaves out the biggest costs of owning (property tax, insurance, maintenance, closing and selling costs) and the biggest advantage of renting (the down payment stays invested). This calculator puts both on the same footing.

Day one: the buyer pays the down payment and closing costs; the renter invests the same amount. Every month: the buyer pays principal and interest, PMI while the loan is above 80% of the purchase price, property tax and maintenance (as a share of the current home value), insurance and HOA dues. The renter pays rent. Whichever costs less that month, that household invests the difference, so both spend identical cash. Home value and rent step up once a year; investments compound monthly. At the end: the buyer sells, pays selling costs and the remaining loan, and keeps the rest plus any investments. The verdict compares the two households' wealth.

What this does not model

Income taxes (the mortgage-interest deduction only helps if you itemize; investment gains may be taxed, while up to $250,000 of home-sale gain, $500,000 for couples, is usually tax-free), renter's insurance, moving costs, refinancing, rent control, a housing downturn, or quality-of-life factors like space, control and stability. Treat the answer as a starting point for the parts you can price.

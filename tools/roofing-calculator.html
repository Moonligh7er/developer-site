Roofing / Shingle Calculator

Enter the building footprint, the overhang and the roof pitch. You get the sloped roof area, squares, shingle bundles, underlayment rolls and nails.

Footprint & pitch

How far the roof sticks out past the walls on each side. 12–24 in is common.
6 means a 6:12 roof (26.6°). Asphalt shingles need at least 2:12, with extra underlayment below 4:12.

Materials

10% simple gable; 15% hip; 20% or more for valleys, dormers and cut-up roofs.

Estimate

Roof area vs. footprint

A sloped roof has more surface than the ground it covers. The pitch multiplier is √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²): 4:12 is 1.054, 6:12 is 1.118, 8:12 is 1.202, 12:12 is 1.414. roof area = (length + 2 × overhang) × (width + 2 × overhang) × multiplier. This holds for gable and hip roofs alike as long as every plane has the same pitch; for mixed pitches, run each section separately.

Squares and bundles

Roofing is measured in squares of 100 sq ft. Most 3-tab and architectural shingles come three bundles to the square (about 33.3 sq ft per bundle); some heavy designer shingles are four or five, so check the wrapper. Bundles are rounded up after the waste factor.

Underlayment and nails

Synthetic underlayment rolls usually cover 10 squares; #15 felt about 4 and #30 felt about 2. Nails: a 3-tab square is 80 shingles (5 in exposure), an architectural square about 65 (5⅝ in exposure), times 4 nails each, or 6 where the maker or local code requires high-wind nailing. Coil nails come about 7,200 to a box.

What this doesn't include

Ridge and hip caps, starter strip, drip edge, ice-and-water membrane at eaves and valleys, flashing and vent boots, and tear-off and disposal. Budget for those separately.

Stair Calculator

Enter the total rise (finished floor to finished floor) and the largest riser you'll accept. The calculator picks the fewest equal risers that stay at or under that height, then checks the result against the IRC residential limits (or IBC commercial limits) and the 2R+T comfort rule.

Stair layout

How the stair math works

risers = ceil(total rise ÷ max riser), so every riser is equal and none is taller than your limit. riser height = total rise ÷ risers. There is one fewer tread than risers because the upper floor is the last step, so total run = (risers − 1) × tread. The stringer estimate is the diagonal √(rise² + run²); buy the next standard board length up and lay out the cuts before trusting it.

Code limits used for the checks

  • IRC R311.7.5 (2021/2024 International Residential Code): riser 7¾ in max, tread 10 in min, nosing ¾–1¼ in required when the tread is under 11 in, and risers or treads in one flight may differ by no more than ⅜ in.
  • IRC R311.7.3: a single flight may rise at most 151 in (12 ft 7 in) before a landing. R311.7.2: headroom at least 6 ft 8 in.
  • IBC 1011.5.2 (commercial): riser between 4 and 7 in, tread 11 in min.

The 2R+T comfort rule

Two risers plus one tread between about 24 and 25 in (610–635 mm) matches a natural walking stride. It is an ergonomic guideline, not a code requirement, so the calculator reports it separately from the code checks.

What's not included

Landings, headroom clearance under the floor above, handrails and guards, stair width, nosing projection, stringer material and spans, and structural attachment. Your local code may amend the model codes above, so check with your building department.

Sources: 2021 and 2024 IRC sections R311.7.2, R311.7.3 and R311.7.5; 2021 IBC section 1011.5.2. Checked November 2026.

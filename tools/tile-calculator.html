Tile Calculator

Enter the area, the tile size and the grout joint. You get tiles and boxes (rounded up), thinset for your trowel size, and grout worked out from the actual joint volume.

Area & tile

Length × width of the floor or wall. Measure each section and add them up.
1/16 in = 0.0625; 1/8 in = 0.125; 3/16 in = 0.1875. Use 0 for click-lock planks.
Used for grout volume. Most floor tile is 5/16–3/8 in (8–10 mm).

Packaging, waste & setting

10% straight lay; 15% diagonal or herringbone; up to 20% for rooms with many cuts.
Auto: 1/4 × 1/4 under 8 in, 1/4 × 3/8 for 8–16 in, 1/2 × 1/2 above 16 in.

How the tile math works

Each tile occupies its own size plus one grout joint in each direction: (width + joint) × (length + joint). tiles = ceil(area ÷ that footprint × (1 + waste)) and boxes = ceil(tiles ÷ tiles per box). The hub page's Flooring / Tile calculator uses the same formula, so both give the same count.

Thinset

Coverage per 50 lb bag depends on the trowel notch. The figures used are the low end of a manufacturer chart (Custom Building Products): about 85–95 sq ft with a 1/4 × 1/4 in square notch and 60–67 sq ft with 1/4 × 3/8 in. Large-format tile with a 1/2 in notch drops to about 40 sq ft. Back-buttering, uneven floors and tile backs all use more; the bag's own chart wins.

Grout

Grout fills the joints, so it is worked out from their volume: kg/m² = (L + W) ÷ (L × W) × joint × thickness × 1.7, with sizes in millimetres and 1.7 kg/dm³ as a typical cement-grout density. Small tiles and wide joints use far more grout than large tiles with tight joints. The waste factor is applied to thinset and grout too, for mixing and cleanup loss.

Buy from one lot

Tile color varies slightly between production lots. Buy the full quantity at once from the same lot, and keep a box for future repairs.

Sources: Custom Building Products thinset coverage charts; standard grout consumption formula used by grout makers. Checked November 2026.
